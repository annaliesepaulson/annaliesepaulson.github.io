I am currently a postdoctoral research associate at the Annenberg Institute for School Reform at Brown University, working with Lindsay Page. In 2025, I completed my PhD at the Center for the Study of Higher and Postsecondary Education in the University of Michigan's Marsal School of Education, studying under Kevin Stange and Lisa Lattuca. In my research, I study the effects of college coursework, the policies that shape course-taking, and how we can support students academically. 

Drawing on tools from data science and causal inference, I combine large scale administrative data from state longitudinal data systems with novel sources of text like syllabi and course catalogs to measure what is taught in college coursework and how it affects students. My work across these domains has been generously supported by more than $1,000,000 in grant funding including a Statistical and Research Methodology in Education Grant from the Institute of Education Sciences, an AERA-NSF research grant, and a Propelling Original Data Science Grant from the Michigan Institute for Data & AI in Society.

I was a 2024 Spencer/National Academy of Education Dissertation Fellow and an Institute of Education Sciences Predoctoral Fellow in Causal Inference in Education Policy Research at the Ford School of Public Policy. Prior to graduate school, I worked as a residence hall director at Lawrence University. I hold an MA in Higher Education from the University of Michigan and a BA in Mathematics and Philosophy from Lawrence University.
